### 2.1 Where the single-multiplicity assumption enters

The Schur-isotropy argument (Step 3, lines 137–144 of `sign_pattern_lemma1_general_S.md`):
$$\|F_a \zeta\|^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}\langle\zeta|\mathbf{F}^2|\zeta\rangle = \tfrac{F(F+1)}{3}, \quad \forall a \in \{x,y,z\} \tag{*}$$
is asserted "for **any** $a$ (state-averaged by $H$-isotropy)". The assertion `⟨F_a^2⟩ = ⟨F^2⟩/3` follows from Schur's lemma applied to the symmetric rank-2 tensor $T_{ab} = \langle \zeta | F_a F_b + F_b F_a | \zeta\rangle / 2$ in the (invariant) subspace generated by $\zeta$ under $H$. **When the invariant subspace under $H$ is 1-dimensional**, $\zeta$ is fixed up to phase, and $T_{ab}$ is constrained to be proportional to $\delta_{ab}$ by $H$-irreducibility of the rank-2 spherical tensor representation in the trivial irrep ($H$ acts trivially on rank-2 scalar $\sum_a F_a^2$ but non-trivially on the traceless rank-2 part, which for $T, O, I$ has no trivial component). Hence Schur isotropy holds.

**When the invariant subspace is 2-dimensional** (the F=9 T:A case), Schur's lemma still constrains a single basis vector $\zeta$ to satisfy $(*)$ only **if $\zeta$ is itself an eigenvector of an $H$-invariant operator restricted to the 2-dim subspace** that is also a Schur-isotropic representative. A generic linear combination $\zeta = \alpha \zeta_1 + \beta \zeta_2$ of an arbitrary orthonormal basis pair $\{\zeta_1, \zeta_2\}$ of the 2-dim invariant subspace will have $\langle \zeta | F_a^2 | \zeta\rangle$ varying with $a$ unless $(\alpha, \beta)$ is in a specific 1-parameter family (or the basis is chosen to diagonalize the relevant operator).

[Established] The numerical evidence corroborates this: `f9_f11_verification_result.md` line 14 reports Schur isotropy deviation `6.04e-14` (≈ machine precision) for the random representative, which means **the specific random pick happened to be very close to Schur-isotropic**, but `β_0` still deviates by `2e-4`. This is the smoking gun that **even at numerical Schur isotropy** the random representative is not the canonical one for which the $1/(2F+1)$ identity holds.

### 2.2 The proof step that fails at multiplicity 2

Quoting `sign_pattern_lemma1_general_S.md` (lines 137–140, Step 3):

> $$\|F_a \zeta\|^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}\langle\zeta|\mathbf{F}^2|\zeta\rangle = \tfrac{F(F+1)}{3}$$
> for **any** $a \in \{x, y, z\}$ (state-averaged by $H$-isotropy).

And the singlet identity (line 14):
$$X_0^{(\rm anom)} = -\frac{1}{2F+1} = \beta_0^{(\lambda_{\rm spin})}$$

The derivation uses (lines 18–19): "(1) Singlet annihilation $(F_a^{(1)} + F_a^{(2)})|0,0\rangle = 0$; (2) Schur isotropy for polyhedral inert states $\|F_a \zeta\|^2 = F(F+1)/3$." Input (2) is what breaks at multiplicity ≥ 2 for a generic representative.

A second, equivalent phrasing: $\beta_0^{(c_0)} = |\langle 0,0 | \zeta \otimes \zeta\rangle|^2$. The singlet $|0,0\rangle$ is the unique $SU(2)$-invariant in $V_F \otimes V_F$, and equals $|0,0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2F+1}} \sum_m (-1)^{F-m} |F,m\rangle|F,-m\rangle$. Then
$$\langle 0,0 | \zeta \otimes \zeta\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2F+1}} \sum_m (-1)^{F-m} \zeta_m \zeta_{-m}$$
For F=9 (odd F, so the parity factor $(-1)^F = -1$), this is the antisymmetric pairing $\sum_m (-1)^{m+1} \zeta_m \zeta_{-m}$, which **vanishes identically** by antisymmetry if $\zeta$ has $\zeta_m = \zeta_{-m}$ (real-symmetric in $m \leftrightarrow -m$); it equals $-1/\sqrt{2F+1}$ in modulus precisely when $\sum_m (-1)^{m+1} \zeta_m \zeta_{-m} = \pm 1$.

**The condition $|\sum_m (-1)^{m+1} \zeta_m \zeta_{-m}|^2 = 1$ is exactly the singlet-overlap-saturation condition**, and it is equivalent to "the symmetric part of $\zeta \otimes \zeta$ has its singlet component exhausting $1/(2F+1)$ of its norm-squared." For a generic random unit vector in a 2-dim invariant subspace this overlap is **NOT 1** in general; it depends on the choice of $(\alpha, \beta)$.

### 2.3 Why the deviation is small (2e-4, not O(1))

[Plausible] The 2e-4 residual rather than an O(1) deviation suggests that **most** unit vectors in the 2-dim invariant subspace are "close to" the canonical Schur-isotropic representative. Seed 1 (`Random.seed!(1)`) happened to produce a $\zeta$ that has $\beta_0 = 0.0524$ vs the exact $1/19 = 0.05263157...$ — a relative error of about $4 \times 10^{-3}$, or absolute error $2 \times 10^{-4}$. This is consistent with the random vector having a small ~$\sqrt{2 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 1.4\%$ admixture of the "wrong" basis vector in the canonical decomposition (the orbit-average projector vs the rank-1 projector differ by $O(\epsilon^2)$ in the relevant quadratic form $\sum_M |c_{S,M}|^2$).

This is **not a numerical conditioning issue** — Schur isotropy is at 6e-14, equivariance at 3e-14 (lines 12–14 of result md). The residual is a representation-theoretic artifact of picking a single representative in a 2-dim space.

### 2.4 Two candidate fixes

#### §2.A — Projector-orbit average (canonical SU(2)-invariant object)

[Plausible] Replace the rank-1 outer product $\zeta \otimes \zeta$ with the **projector onto the 2-dim invariant subspace**, divided by its dimension (so it has unit trace per dim):
$$\hat{P}_{\rm inv} = \zeta_1 \zeta_1^\dagger + \zeta_2 \zeta_2^\dagger, \qquad \rho_{\rm inv} = \frac{1}{d_{\rm rep}} \hat{P}_{\rm inv} = \frac{1}{2} \hat{P}_{\rm inv}$$
where $\{\zeta_1, \zeta_2\}$ is **any** orthonormal basis of $\mathrm{Im}(P_{\rm T:A})$ (which is the image of the irrep-A projector restricted to $V_F$; its dimension equals the multiplicity $m_{\rm T:A}(F)$ — for F=9 T:A this is 2).

The orbit-averaged $\beta_0$ becomes:
$$\bar{\beta}_0^{(c_0)} = \frac{1}{d_{\rm rep}} \sum_{i=1}^{d_{\rm rep}} |\langle 0,0 | \zeta_i \otimes \zeta_i \rangle|^2 + (\text{cross terms in $\bar\rho \otimes \bar\rho$})$$

The cleanest definition is to **lift to density-matrix form**:
$$\bar{\beta}_S^{(c_0)} \equiv \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[ \hat{\Pi}_S \cdot (\rho_{\rm inv} \otimes \rho_{\rm inv}) \right]$$
where $\hat{\Pi}_S = \sum_M |S,M\rangle\langle S,M|$ is the channel-S projector in $V_F \otimes V_F$. Since $\rho_{\rm inv}$ is the $H$-invariant projector normalized so $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\rm inv}) = 1$, and $\hat\Pi_S$ is $SU(2)$-invariant (hence also $H$-invariant), the trace is the canonical, basis-independent quantity.

[Plausible] By Schur's lemma applied to the **2-dim trivial-irrep multiplicity space** (which carries a trivial action of $H$, since both basis vectors are $H$-invariant), the average $\rho_{\rm inv} = \tfrac{1}{2}(|\zeta_1\rangle\langle\zeta_1| + |\zeta_2\rangle\langle\zeta_2|)$ is **independent of the choice of orthonormal basis** of the multiplicity space. Hence $\bar{\beta}_S^{(c_0)}$ is canonical (seed-independent in the implementation).

[Plausible] Predicted endpoint: at $S=0$, using that $|0,0\rangle$ is the unique $SU(2)$-singlet:
$$\bar{\beta}_0^{(c_0)} = \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[ |0,0\rangle\langle 0,0| \cdot (\rho_{\rm inv} \otimes \rho_{\rm inv}) \right] = \langle 0,0 | \rho_{\rm inv} \otimes \rho_{\rm inv} | 0,0\rangle$$

Since $\rho_{\rm inv}$ commutes with all $g \in H$ and the trivial $H$-action on its image is one-dimensional per basis vector, $\rho_{\rm inv} \otimes \rho_{\rm inv}$ acts on the singlet (which is the unique $SU(2)$-trivial — hence in particular $H$-trivial — vector in $V_F \otimes V_F$) **proportionally to its overlap with the symmetric square** $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathrm{Im}\, P_{\rm T:A})$.

For polyhedral inert state cases where the $H$-trivial irrep occurs with multiplicity $m$ in $V_F$ AND the $SU(2)$-singlet $|0,0\rangle$ is in $\mathrm{Im}(P_{\rm T:A}) \otimes \mathrm{Im}(P_{\rm T:A})$ (which it must be, since the $SU(2)$-singlet is $H$-trivial), the calculation reduces to a finite linear-algebra problem on an $m^2$-dimensional space. **The predicted value is $1/(2F+1)$ if and only if the canonical orbit-average reproduces the Schur-isotropy norm condition** — and we expect it to, by the following dimension count:

$\rho_{\rm inv}$ has $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\rm inv} \cdot \mathbf{F}^2) / \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\rm inv}) = F(F+1)$ (acting on the trivial irrep, $\mathbf{F}^2$ has eigenvalue $F(F+1)$ on $V_F$, and this is just the average over the 2-dim subspace, both vectors lying in $V_F$). So the orbit-averaged Schur isotropy:
$$\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\rm inv} F_a^2) = \tfrac{1}{3} \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\rm inv} \mathbf{F}^2) = \tfrac{F(F+1)}{3}$$
exactly, **by symmetry of the orbit average** (the operator $F_a^2 - \mathbf{F}^2/3$ is traceless rank-2 in $a$, and its $H$-average over the 2-dim trivial-irrep multiplicity space vanishes — this is the key Schur step). This restores condition $(*)$ exactly for $\rho_{\rm inv}$.

#### §2.B — Schur-isotropic basis selection (canonical representative)

[Plausible] Pick the orthonormal basis $\{\zeta_1, \zeta_2\}$ such that each individual $\zeta_i$ satisfies Schur isotropy $\langle \zeta_i | F_a^2 | \zeta_i\rangle = F(F+1)/3$ AND $\langle \zeta_i | F_a F_b | \zeta_i\rangle = 0$ for $a \neq b$. Existence: such a basis exists because the rank-2 traceless symmetric quadratic form $Q_{ab}(\zeta) = \langle \zeta | F_a F_b + F_b F_a | \zeta\rangle / 2 - \delta_{ab} F(F+1)/3$ restricted to the 2-dim invariant subspace is a (real-quadratic-in-$\zeta$) map $\mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^5$ (5 components of a symmetric traceless 3x3 matrix), and a non-trivial null vector is guaranteed by generic arguments only when the map is not injective — in practice, the null variety of $Q$ in $S^3$ (unit 2-dim complex sphere) is typically 1-dimensional, giving a $U(1)$-family of representatives.

Concretely: build the 2-dim subspace from the projector $P$ via two random seeds (orthogonalize), get $\{e_1, e_2\}$. Form the rank-2 traceless symmetric tensor $T_{ab}(\alpha) = \langle e(\alpha) | F_a F_b + F_b F_a | e(\alpha)\rangle / 2 - \delta_{ab} F(F+1)/3$ where $e(\alpha) = \cos\alpha\, e_1 + e^{i\phi}\sin\alpha\, e_2$. Solve $T_{ab} = 0$ for $(\alpha, \phi)$ — this is 5 real equations in 2 real unknowns, generically over-determined, but **the polyhedral residual symmetry constraints reduce the number of independent equations**: $H$-invariance forces $T_{ab}$ to vanish in all components except those compatible with the rank-2 trivial representation of $H$, which for $T, O, I$ has **no trivial component in the traceless rank-2 part** (= the very reason mult-1 cases enjoy automatic Schur isotropy). So at mult 2, $T_{ab}$ has zero $H$-irreducible non-trivial components by group-theoretic constraint — **the system reduces to a small number of equations soluble by 1-parameter family**.

#### §2.C — Are §2.A and §2.B equivalent?

[Plausible] **Yes, in the sense that both predict $\beta_0 = 1/(2F+1)$ exactly**, but they are NOT computing the same quantity:
- §2.A computes a **scalar averaged over the multiplicity space** — a density-matrix quantity.
- §2.B picks a **single representative vector** that satisfies Schur isotropy individually.

The relation: if $\{\zeta_1, \zeta_2\}$ is a Schur-isotropic orthonormal basis (in the §2.B sense), then each individual $\zeta_i$ gives $\beta_0(\zeta_i) = 1/(2F+1)$, and their average $\bar\beta_0 = 1/(2F+1)$ trivially. **Conversely**, the orbit average $\bar\beta_S$ computed from any orthonormal basis equals the §2.B-canonical $\beta_S$ for **all** S (by basis-independence of the trace formulation in §2.A). So §2.A is **strictly more canonical** (no $U(1)$-ambiguity in $\zeta_i$ choice) and §2.B is a witnessing single-vector construction.

**Recommendation**: implementer should code §2.A (projector-orbit / density-matrix formulation) because:
1. No $U(1)$ phase ambiguity to resolve numerically.
2. Generalizes to multiplicity $m \geq 3$ trivially.
3. Computes ALL $\beta_S$ in one shot via the matrix trace.

§2.B is useful as a **theory check**: verify that picking any §2.B-canonical $\zeta_1$ alone gives $\beta_0 = 1/19$ exactly — this corroborates the multiplicity diagnosis from a second, independent angle.
